---
title: 'Knocking the Dust Off It'
description:
  I haven't written anything substantial for my overengineered personal site
  since June of last year, and I want to get back to doing something uniquely
  human by expressing my thoughts without leaning on artificial intelligence.
published: 2026-11-15T17:40:00+0000
tags: ['Skills: Soft']
---

When I first created my personal site and started writing mostly how-to articles
about somewhat niche technical things, there were esentially two motivators:

1. Given my goldfish brain, I wanted to leave myself a trail of breadcrumbs to
   remember how the hell I did some moderately involved technical thing.
2. I wanted to try and make myself more visible online in an attempt to improve
   my job prospects.

I still try and jot down the steps I've taken to replicate a technical task to
remind future me how I did it, and to aid in my own personal knowledge
retention. However, large language models have gotten so good at spitting out a
response to questions like "How do I do XYZ?" that it's less important for me to
share that kind of information online for others to read and follow. You'll
probably get a better outcome using the LLM than my old crusty post from several
years ago, to be honest. _And_ you can ask it follow-up questions, whereas all I
can say if you go trying to contact me online and hope for a timely response is
"Good luck." (Aside from if you're one of my colleagues at work, of course.)

Speaking more to the second point, I'm incredibly satisfied with the
organization I work for and feel great about the mission it serves, so I'm no
longer putting effort into a quest to set myself apart individually from a sea
of qualified engineers to woo prospective employers. I've become more dedicated
to the work of making myself vital to that organization specifically. It's kind
of like when you find your life partner - rather than trying to put yourself out
there and make yourself visible to a wide swath of people to impress a potential
mate, you start putting more effort into the activities that will make your
significant other's life better. You focus on the things that make you more
valuable and desirable to that someone in particular. That's not to say that I'm
as invested in the organization I currently work at as I am in my wife and kids'
lives, but hopefully you get the point I'm getting at.

So if the original reasons I had for writing on this site are no longer
particularly compelling for me, that might prompt you to ask "Why is Jack Barry
back here then?" To which I would respond: because I have thoughts. I have
thoughts that I struggle to express in any other format but to write them out
and look at them against a previously blank page. It's in the wrestling with
those thoughts to solidify them into words and phrases that I come to better
terms with them. Given how long it's been since I've done that in a public way,
I have had many a thought that simply drifted off into the ether never to be
re-visited again, but others that have nagged and persisted through it all. So I
chose to start writing here again. Whether or not my thoughts actually belong in
the public square or whether they're of any value to anyone other than myself is
another question.

That all said, the first pesky thought I have is that the flood of AI slop that
is overrunning the digital world is soul-sucking. It's spilling into the
physical world even, manifesting itself in gaudy posters and godawful clip-art
mash-ups on banners so hideous in their attempted recreation of the things they
are born from that even the creature from John Carpenter's The Thing might find
them repulsive. The reason it bothers me is not so much that it's ugly, which it
is, but it's the question of "Why?" That question that young children will
pester you to death with, that I think many adults forget how to ask, leads me
to hypothetical conversations that I can't begin to understand.

> "We need to have some unique art to show to people to get their attention for
> our event/product/etc."
>
> "Cool, let's find a graphic designer who has a good eye for that kind of
> thing."
>
> "I don't think we need to pay anybody for this, let's just shovel a prompt
> into an AI and get something on the cheap."
>
> "But, _why_? Will people actually resonate with that artwork emotionally and
> correlate that emotion with the thing we're trying to promote?"
>
> "We don't care about that. Marketing has to have pictures, and we just need
> something to fill in the empty space."
>
> "_Why_? Does the empty space actually need to be filled, or do we think so
> lowly of our audience that we think they'll find pictures more convincing than
> a good product/event/etc.?"
>
> "People are sheep with short attention spans, they need a splashy image in
> front of their face to be tricked into paying attention to our thing."
>
> "But _why_ do we operate like that? Do we really want to prey on that
> shortsightedness and exacerbate the lack of foresight in people?"
>
> "We don't care about people other than what they have to offer us by way of
> their purchasing power. We want them to become dependent on us for what we
> sell."
>
> "But _why_ though?"
>
> "Because we want to sell something to them and make money."
>
> "Sure, everyone needs to make a living, but why can't we just make our product
> _better_ so that it makes their lives _better_ in some meaningful way?"
>
> "..."

Round and round it goes. And yes, I know that's not exactly Aaron Sorkin-level
dialog. But do we really feel like this is the best humanity can do,
collectively? To offload our cognition to companies and artificial intelligence
for the sake of enriching a handful of wealthy people with little regard for the
long term consequences of their technology on our species and the world at
large?

Don't read this as me saying "AI bad, never use AI, **ever**!" I am actually
quite keen on finding ways to leverage this technology to _improve_ our ability
to find truthful answers and do more comprehensive and rigorous research, to
answer questions that will enhance peoples' ability to _help_ one another and
make life better for us all. I am not anti-AI; I don't feel threatened by _it_
so much as a frustration with the way it is being implemented as a monkey's paw
scenario, wherein companies give you what you apparently asked for, and it is
not until we have all fallen into the pit of accelerated sloppification and
enshittification that we realize what was actually being asked of us in return,
that is, to give up our humanity in favor of a machine that gives you exactly
what you prompt it for.

That brings me to my messy distillation of this thought: **The _messiness_ of
human expression is paramount to the beauty of it.** When I get mealy-mouthed
and tongue-tied and have to back myself out of the corner to try and find common
ground, to communicate effectively with those around me, I stumble upon the
comedy, the drama, the thrill of being alive. It's in those moments of
unpredictability that I'm roused from my digitally induced fog and reminded that
this ride isn't on autopilot. We have autonomy, we have agency, we have a
choice. The next word you or I say is not inevitable, it's not all just a
regurgitation of what came before and what could be ingested into the machine
prior to this moment. There are novel things, there are novel moments, there are
novel relationships between us constantly revealing themselves if only we let
them.

If you're anything like me, your eyes may glaze over as soon as you realize what
you're reading or viewing is generated slop. The level of sophistication of the
trickery of the slop isn't impressive to me, it's an indicator that someone out
there wants to rid us of our humanity and will spend a lot of time and effort
doing so. With that all in mind, I've intentionally chosen _not_ to use AI tools
to write my posts here, for the time being. I'm going to get the syntax and
grammar wrong. I'm going to spell things in ways that phonetically make sense
but that would irk an English professor. I'm choosing to make mistakes in front
of you, if only so that you would know that I am human and fallible just like
you are. Why would I want a machine to take over that and write or alter my
thoughts into something other than what the written word was meant to be:
asynchronous communication from one living person to another so that they might
see what the author has seen?
